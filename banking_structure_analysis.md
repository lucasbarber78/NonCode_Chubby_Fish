## QuickBooks Online Business Unit Tracking Options

QuickBooks Online doesn't have a dedicated "business units" feature, but offers several tracking options that can be used together to provide business unit functionality. These tracking methods interact with banking structures in different ways:

1. **Classes and Locations**
   - Allow transactions to be tagged without requiring separate bank accounts
   - Classes can track transactions at line-item level (good for departments) 
   - Locations apply to entire transactions (good for physical locations)
   - Both work with any banking structure, from single account to multiple accounts

2. **Bank Feed Rules**
   - Can automatically categorize transactions to specific classes/locations
   - Works based on transaction details from bank feeds
   - Makes reconciliation more efficient with multiple accounts
   - Helps maintain consistent categorization of recurring transactions

3. **Bank Account Registers**
   - QuickBooks limits each bank account to one register
   - Cannot subdivide bank accounts internally without using classes/locations
   - Each real-world bank account requires a separate QuickBooks account

4. **Inter-Account Transfers**
   - QuickBooks can track transfers between bank accounts
   - Useful when using multiple accounts for different business units
   - Provides audit trail of fund movements between business units

## Potential Current Situations

**Scenario 1: Single Account**
- One operating account for all businesses
- Revenue and expenses tracked through classes/departments in QuickBooks
- Pros: Simplicity, easier cash flow management
- Cons: More difficult to track profitability by business unit, potential commingling issues

**Scenario 2: Basic Separation**
- Separate operating accounts for each business (Chubby Fish, Seahorse, Airbnb)
- Shared accounts for other functions (payroll, taxes, etc.)
- Pros: Better tracking of business unit performance
- Cons: More accounts to reconcile

**Scenario 3: Comprehensive Structure**
- Separate legal entities with distinct accounts for each business
- Dedicated accounts for specific functions (operating, payroll, taxes, capital)
- Pros: Clean legal separation, accurate profitability tracking
- Cons: Most complex to manage, may be overkill for their size

## Industry Best Practices

### Recommended Structure for Restaurant Groups
1. **Separate Operating Accounts** for each business unit or revenue center
   - Enables accurate profitability analysis
   - Prevents commingling of funds between different concepts
   - Simplifies accounting and tax preparation

2. **Dedicated Payroll Account**
   - Segregates payroll funds from general operating expenses
   - Easier to budget for labor costs
   - Can be a single account with allocation to different businesses

3. **Tax Reserve Account**
   - Holds funds for sales tax, hospitality tax, and liquor tax
   - Prevents accidental spending of tax money
   - Ensures taxes are paid on time

4. **Capital/Investment Account**
   - Dedicated account for major purchases or renovations
   - Keeps expansion projects separate from day-to-day operations
   - Helps track return on investment for new initiatives

5. **Owner's Draw/Distribution Account** (if applicable)
   - Separates business finances from personal
   - Creates clear record of distributions to owners
